# Source atlas design

Reading this as a research desk for readers comparing Syria sources, in an editorial atlas style. ENERGY 1 / RHYTHM 2 / MOTION 1.

- Warm paper and deep green support long reading; rust marks selection and qualifications rather than political loyalty.
- Georgia gives titles a publication character; system sans-serif keeps dense controls and profile evidence legible without remote fonts.
- A wide graph and adjacent inspector connect overview to evidence; the directory supplies a complete text alternative on smaller screens.
- Constant node sizes and labelled connections avoid invented influence and credibility metrics.
- Rectangular sources and rounded research themes distinguish entity types independently of colour.
- Thin rules and structural spacing separate records without ornamental shadows or floating cards.
- Motion is limited to optional anchor scrolling and hover/focus feedback; reduced-motion preferences disable smooth scrolling.
- Theme switching serves different reading environments and does not persist personal data.
- Source profiles expose evidence and caveats before comparison; comparisons preserve distinct roles rather than ranking sources.
